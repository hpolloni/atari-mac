# REQ-116: NMI input

## Requirement

The emulator shall treat NMI as edge triggered and not masked by `I`, and shall service each edge once with a 7-cycle sequence that loads `PC` from `$FFFA`.

## Rationale

ANTIC sends the display list interrupt (DLI) and the vertical blank interrupt (VBI) as NMIs (HRM 4.8). The OS needs the VBI in each frame.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Start: `PC` = `$0200`, `NOP` instructions from `$0200`, `I` = 1, `P` = `$24`, `S` = `$FF`, `$FFFA` = `$00`, `$FFFB` = `$40`. The NMI line goes high in cycle 1 (the first cycle of the `NOP` at `$0200`). The NMI sequence runs in cycles 3 to 9. It writes `$02` to `$01FF`, `$01` to `$01FE` and `$24` to `$01FD`. It reads `$FFFA` in cycle 8 and `$FFFB` in cycle 9. After it, `PC` = `$4000` and `I` = 1.
2. Start: as in criterion 1, with `NOP` instructions at `$4000`. The NMI line stays high for 1,000 cycles. The CPU runs one NMI sequence: it reads `$FFFA` only once.
3. Start: as in criterion 2. The NMI line goes high in cycle 1, low in cycle 2, and high again in cycle 3 (the first cycle of the NMI sequence). It then stays high. The CPU runs one NMI sequence in 1,000 cycles.

## Notes

The HRM does not give the NMI vector address. MCS6500-HW App-A (A.5.4) and MCS6500-PRG 9.10 give the NMI vector at `$FFFA` and `$FFFB`.

MCS6500-HW 1.4.1.2.9 says that NMI responds to a transition, that the I flag cannot mask it, and that the CPU detects no other NMI until the line goes back and changes again. MCS6500-PRG 9.10 says that the NMI sequence is the same as the IRQ sequence, except for the vector.
