# REQ-411: Composite artifacting

## Requirement

The emulator shall have an optional display filter that shows false colors for alternating high resolution pixel patterns, and the filter shall be off by default.

## Rationale

Some NTSC programs use artifacting to get colors in hires modes. The HRM states that artifacting is an effect of the composite video decoder, not of GTIA. The most common XE colors are red and blue.

## Acceptance criteria

In these criteria, row A is 384 frame buffer bytes `$0E`, `$00`, `$0E`, `$00`, ... (a mode F line of `$AA` bytes with `COLPF2` = `$00` and `COLPF1` = `$0E`). Row B is 384 bytes `$00`, `$0E`, `$00`, `$0E`, ... (`$55` bytes). The mean color of a row is the mean R, G and B of output columns 16-367. "Luma" is `0.299 R + 0.587 G + 0.114 B`.

1. With the default frontend settings, the RGB value of each frame buffer byte b is `palette::rgb(standard, b)` (the filter is off).
2. With the filter on, the mean color of row A and the mean color of row B each have `max(R, G, B) - min(R, G, B)` of 32 or more.
3. With the filter on, the mean colors of row A and row B differ by 32 or more in at least one channel.
4. With the filter on, the luma of the mean color of row A and of row B is 119 ± 34 (half of luminance 14, ± 2 luminance steps).
5. With the filter on, a row of 384 bytes `$94` gives `palette::rgb(NTSC, $94)` in columns 16-367, with a tolerance of 2 for each channel.

## Notes

This is a TV effect. The frame buffer (REQ-405) does not change. The filter works on the RGB output in the frontend. The HRM gives the brightness of artifact colors as the average of the two pixels (HRM 6.4). The thresholds 32 and ± 34 are project decisions. The criteria do not specify the hues (HRM Table 118 gives a hue delta of 238° for the 130XE). The criteria cover NTSC only. The requirement and the Source also name PAL artifacting (HRM D.5), which the design does not have.
