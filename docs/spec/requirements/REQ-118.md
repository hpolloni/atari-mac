# REQ-118: Interrupt recognition delay

## Requirement

The emulator shall start an interrupt sequence no earlier than 2 CPU cycles after the cycle in which the interrupt line is asserted.

## Rationale

ANTIC pulls NMI at cycle 8. The 6502 needs two cycles to acknowledge the NMI, so the earliest NMI sequence starts at cycle 10 (HRM 4.8). DLI timing and POKEY timer timing depend on this delay.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The CPU executes `NOP` instructions. A `NOP` runs in cycles N and N+1. The NMI line goes high in cycle N. The NMI sequence starts in cycle N+2.
2. A `NOP` runs in cycles N-1 and N. The NMI line goes high in cycle N. The next `NOP` runs in cycles N+1 and N+2. The NMI sequence starts in cycle N+3.
3. With `I` = 0 and the IRQ line asserted in cycle N instead of the NMI line, criteria 1 and 2 give the same cycles for the IRQ sequence.

## Notes

The HRM states the 2-cycle delay for NMI (HRM 4.8). For IRQ, HRM 5.7 states a delay of 2-3 cycles from `IRQST` to the start of the sequence. The CPU uses the 2-cycle rule for IRQ, and the POKEY design must give the remaining delay (project decision). REQ-127 covers halted cycles.

MCS6500-HW 1.4.1.2.9 says that the NMI signal must be low for at least two clock cycles for the CPU to recognize the interrupt. This agrees with the 2-cycle delay.
